# Site Designer — User Guide

**Audience:** authors using the Site designer (`/admin/designer`). This is
the seed of the product's "How to use this page" copy; the dev-facing design
doc is [site-designer.md](site-designer.md).

The Site designer shows your real site — a live build of your actual books,
posts, and bio — beside a rail of design choices. Change anything and the
preview rebuilds in a couple of seconds. Nothing you pick is published until
you save; while the designer is in preview, your choices are kept in this
browser only.

---

## Your brand, in three rungs

Your site's identity in the header climbs a ladder. Start at the bottom;
climb only as far as you need:

1. **The pairing's display face (the default).** Your site title is set in
   the display font of whatever font pairing you chose. Pick a pairing that
   fits your genre and your title already looks right — most authors never
   need more than this.
2. **A wordmark font** *(coming)*: the same site title, set in a face with
   more flavor than your pairing's display — a script, a marker, a slab.
   This styles *only* the title; the rest of the site keeps your pairing.
3. **A logo image (the top rung).** Upload your own logo and it replaces
   the site title in the header entirely.

Why the ladder is shaped this way: **a logo is brand, not typography.** A
wordmark is a fixed asset you choose once — not a system font that flows
through the page. Body text, headings, and captions should come from one
carefully-made pairing (that's what keeps every page coherent); the one
place that deserves its own voice is the mark that *is* you. So the
designer keeps typography to a heading + body pairing, and gives your brand
its own dedicated slot at the top of the header.

### The logo slot

- Drag an image onto the logo box (or use the button). JPG, PNG, AVIF,
  WebP, or SVG up to 5 MB; at least 100 pixels tall reads well.
- Your **site title stays as the image's alt text** — screen readers and
  image-off browsers still get your name.
- Prefer the title visible *beside* the logo? Untick "Site title as alt
  only": the title appears to the right of the logo in the Split header
  and below it in the centered layouts, set in your display face.
- The logo saves to your account immediately (it's the same logo as System
  settings).

## The header

- **Layout**: four arrangements — Split (brand left, links right),
  Centered, Grand (marquee-sized brand), and Bare (links only, no brand;
  a Home link appears automatically since the brand isn't there to click).
- **Links**: rename Books, Posts, and About, hide any of them, reorder
  with the arrows, and add your own links to anywhere on the web. Built-in
  links show their destination so you always know where they lead.
- **Header button**: the highlighted button at the end of the nav. Three
  choices — keep the default newsletter sign-up, replace it with your own
  label and link (optionally opening in a new tab), or switch it off
  entirely.

## Color palettes and light/dark mode

Fifteen palettes, named and grouped by the shelf they suit — from Nebula's
deep-space gold to Pine's quiet literary teal. **Every palette carries a
designed light and dark rendition**; the Mode control at the top of the
palette picker chooses Light, Dark, or Auto — Auto follows each reader's
own system preference, automatically.

Want your own colors? **Pick your colors** opens a color wheel: choose a
hue for Background, Accent, and Text (or type any hex). You pick the
*color*; the exact shades are set for you, per role and per mode, so your
text stays readable no matter what you choose — in both light and dark.

## Button rounding

A Site-styles slider takes every button on your site from square to fully
rounded — the same four-stop scale as book-cover corners. Out of the box,
buttons follow the palette's own character; the slider overrides it.

## The books section

- **Show on home page** — switch the whole books section off your home
  page. Your books and series pages stay published and linked in the
  header; only the home-page section hides.
- **Show only the first book of each series** — or switch it off to show
  every book as its own card.
- **Alternate sides row by row** — row layouts zig-zag covers left and
  right, or keep them uniform.
- **Seven layouts**: Spread (roomy rows), Shelf (compact rows with
  dividers), Grid (cover wall), Panel (framed cards), Carousel (a
  draggable, swipeable band), Offset shadow (floating covers), and Cover
  fan (a series' covers fanned like a hand of cards, book one in front).
- **Cover corners** — a slider from square to rounded, applied to every
  book image on the site.

## Font pairings

Each pairing is a heading face and a body face chosen to work together,
named for the shelf it belongs on — Epic, Thriller, Noir, Romance, Gothic,
Storybook, Ledger, and more. Every card in the picker is drawn in its own
faces with a one-line note on what it suits. Twenty-one pairings cover the
major fiction genres plus memoir, nonfiction, children's, and poetry.

Fonts are cheap here: choosing an ornate pairing doesn't slow your site
down — readers download only the two faces your site actually uses.

Prefer your own? **Pick your own fonts** below the pairings accepts any
Google Font for headings and body. A custom heading face arrives with a
clean slate — no all-caps, letterspacing, or weight inherited from
whatever pairing you were on before. Your picks become a **Custom card**
at the end of the pairing list, so trying other pairings never throws
them away — click the card to come back. (The card lives in this browser,
like the rest of your unsaved design.)
